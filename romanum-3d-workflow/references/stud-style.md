# Stud style on rounded shapes

The owner calls these two treadmills a perfect example of the stud style. The rule they show: **the model keeps its own smooth, rounded or bevelled shapes, and the studs are only a texture on its surfaces.** The asset is not built out of studs or blocks.

| Image | Study it for |
| --- | --- |
| [Candy treadmill](../assets/references/stud-style/candy-treadmill.png) | Curved candy canes, round lollipops and a bevelled deck, all smooth shapes, with a fine stud texture on the faces |
| [Ice treadmill](../assets/references/stud-style/ice-treadmill.png) | Bevelled rails, a curved handrail and sharp ice crystals, with the same studs pressed into the surfaces |

## What to copy

- Model the silhouette first as a normal stylised prop: curves stay curves, bevels stay soft, points stay sharp. Never step a curve into blocks to make it look "studded".
- Add the studs as a texture or normal detail on top, following the surface. The studs are small next to the object and evenly spaced, so they read as a material, like fabric on a toy.
- Keep stud size the same across one asset and across neighbouring assets.
- Leave the important read clean: the belt arrows, the screen and the glow stay readable over the studs. Studs can be quieter on busy or small parts.
- Colour and glow carry the theme (pink and white candy, pale cyan ice). The studs add texture, not colour.

## What not to do

- Do not model studs as geometry, or build the asset from cubes, voxels or stacked bricks.
- Do not use a blocky silhouette because the style has studs in it.
- Do not draw or generate a fake stud pattern into the base colour. Use the project's actual stud texture, as the [UI workflow's stud texture rule](../../romanum-ui-workflow/references/asset-extraction-and-assembly.md#stud-texture-layer) says.

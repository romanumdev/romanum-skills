# Roblox player onboarding

Begin with the intended audience, device and the first action that demonstrates the game's appeal. Public players/visits cannot reveal tutorial completion, retention or session duration. Use authorised first-party data or explicit playtest observations; otherwise propose instrumentation without inventing a diagnosis.

## Observe before prescribing

Map entry -> control discovery -> first action -> visible reward -> next voluntary goal. Mark each step as observed, measured or assumed. Check loading/performance, navigation, control confusion and progression pacing alongside the core loop; a low average play time does not identify a single cause.

For younger or early-reading audiences, demonstrate one action at a time, use legible symbols and immediate feedback, and let the player act rather than read a wall of instructions. Test touch targets and camera behaviour on the intended device. Support audio/visual alternatives and reduced intense effects. Do not assume that more stimulation improves learning.

Prefer a short highlight, arrow or pointing cue on the actual next control or world object, with only a few words when needed. Advance when the player performs the action, then remove the cue and show its result. Keep the target usable on touch and controller; offer a brief text or audio explanation when a pointer alone would be ambiguous. Avoid several competing arrows or a tutorial that advances while the player is still confused.

Record a funnel with explicit denominators, cohort dates and event definitions. Avoid player identifiers in a design brief. Where private analytics are unavailable, ask a tester to attempt the first loop and explain what they think happens next. Report observations without generalising a handful of testers to all players.

Use the funnel to locate player drop-off. Define an observable event for each step, count each eligible player or session consistently, and state which unit is used. Show step-to-step completion and loss, plus overall completion from entry; repeated events should not inflate progression. Distinguish loading failures from exits during play and compare device and acquisition cohorts when there is enough data. Investigate a large drop through observation before assigning a cause.

## Small experiments

Choose one friction point and write: changing X may improve Y because of observed Z. Name the primary metric, baseline, comparison window and a guardrail such as errors, frustration or purchase confusion. Keep simultaneous changes limited enough to interpret; compare similar traffic/device cohorts. Retention is affected by more than onboarding, so distinguish correlation from a causal test.

A successful first session teaches a satisfying action and a meaningful next choice. Voluntary return goals, forgiving recovery and optional social play are preferable to streak punishment, deceptive scarcity or repeated purchase prompts. Do not optimise young players' spending or inability to stop as a success metric.
